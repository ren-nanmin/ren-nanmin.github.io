About REN

Refugee Empowerment Network (REN) is a registered non-profit organization that networks refugees, asylum seekers, returnees, resettled refugees, internally displaced people, and their supporters together, supporting their economic and social self-sufficiency and protecting their dignity as people (empowerment).

How REN began

In 2002, REN translated and published in Japan Mama Kakuma, a collection of poems made by refugees living in the Kakuma refugee camp in northern Kenya. With that book, REN began work to tell Japan about the realities of refugee life in refugees' own words.

Afterward, REN built a network with support organizations in Japan and abroad and worked with them. As direct support for refugees in Japan, it started a self-reliance program in which refugees make and sell beaded accessories. It then began holding a regular Japanese class. Based on refugees' needs, REN has added its support activities one by one.

From then until now, REN has been run entirely by volunteers.

REN began in 2003 as the "Kanebu Support Group," formed after publishing Mama Kakuma, a poetry collection written by refugees in Kenya's Kakuma camp. It was renamed Refugee Empowerment Network (REN) in May 2007, and certified as a non-profit organization in September 2009.

Every Wednesday from 13:30–15:30, REN runs Japanese-language and yoga classes for refugees, alongside a beadwork exhibition and sales of African textile goods.

Our approach

Supporting refugees' independence and protecting their dignity

REN places importance on support that respects each refugee's will, opinions, and feelings.

Many refugees living in Japan have lost any place to belong, in the most extreme sense. Blocked by systemic barriers, many people near us cannot even have the bare minimum needed to live: food, clothing, and shelter.

But they are not pitiable people. They are neighbors with potential who have overcome many hardships to reach Japan.

We listen closely to their voices. Through our activities and a place to belong, we aim to help them regain their pride and confidence, and connect with society while fully using their potential.

Main activities

  1. 1. Bead Project and African Fabric Products Project

    Together with refugees, REN makes and sells beaded accessories woven on looms, African fabric products, and more, and uses the proceeds for its support activities. It is an effort in which refugees support refugees.

  2. 2. Cultural exchange (Japanese class)

    REN holds exchange events and community activities with the goal that Japanese people and refugees share their cultures, accept their differences, and respect diversity. As a first step toward living together, it has also held a Japanese class since 2013, to learn the language. After a pause during the pandemic, the class fully reopened in May 2022. It welcomes everyone who comes, from infants to adults. It is more than a language class. REN keeps building a place where refugees can connect with each other and help each other grow. Today it has grown into a multinational community, mostly from Africa and the Middle East. Each weekly session draws participants from more than 19 countries, 35 people on average.

  3. 3. Scholarship support

    Each year, REN gives a small scholarship for higher education to about two refugee students, so that one day they can build careers in their home countries.

  4. 4. Consultation and expert partnerships

    We listen to difficulties in daily life and worries about systems and, as needed, connect people with experts and related organizations. What we can do on our own is very little. For that reason, we are building cooperation with many pro bono professionals and other support organizations.
